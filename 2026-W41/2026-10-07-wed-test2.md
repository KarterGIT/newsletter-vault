## Technical: frontier models
- [ ] keep · **A practical guide to building with GPT-6**: OpenAI, Oct 2, 2026 `[anchor]`
  - GPT-6 family released Oct 2: Astra (top model, $10 in / $50 out per million tokens), Sol (near-Astra at about one-fifth the cost), Luna (fast and cheap, $0.10 / $0.50).
  - Built for tasks lasting hours or days, computer use, and multi-agent workflows.
  - Prompt caching can cut input costs by up to 95%. Details come from OpenAI's developer guide, not a full model card.
  - Jargon: prompt caching = reusing text the model has already read so repeat requests cost less; computer use = AI operating websites and apps like a person
  - https://openai.com/index/practical-guide-building-gpt-6/
- [ ] keep · **AI tools flood underground cybercrime markets**: Axios, Oct 6, 2026 `[anchor]`
  - Halcyon's research team (led by ex-FBI cyber deputy Cynthia Kaiser) counted ads for AI hacking tools rising from under 50 a month in late 2025 to 1,400+ by February 2026.
  - Study covered ~4,000 posts across Telegram channels, dark web forums and markets. Stolen ChatGPT Plus accounts sold for as little as 10 cents.
  - Four tool types: jailbroken AI services, phishing and fraud kits, models trained on malicious data (e.g. WormGPT), AI-boosted malware. No evidence yet of fully autonomous AI attacks.
  - Jargon: jailbreak = a prompt or hack that gets a chatbot to ignore its safety rules
  - https://www.axios.com/2026/10/06/ai-cybercrime-hacker-tools-marketplace

## Technical: hardware
- [ ] keep · **Fed's Daly: AI chip demand could keep inflation higher for longer**: Axios, Oct 6, 2026 `[anchor]`
  - Mary Daly (San Francisco Fed president) says AI demand may spill into wider chip markets before supply catches up, competing with cars and appliances as in the post-pandemic shortage.
  - Fed normally expects supply shocks to fade in 1–3 years; she says relief is "further out" this time. Companies are locking in memory chips with forward contracts.
  - Big AI buyers are less sensitive to interest rates, which weakens the Fed's tools. She backed September's rate hike.
  - Jargon: supply shock = sudden shortage of a key input that pushes prices up
  - https://www.axios.com/2026/10/06/fed-ai-daly-inflation-chips
- [ ] keep · **OpenAI and Synopsys build "GPT-Synopsys" to run chip-design software**: Tom's Hardware, Oct 6, 2026 `[anchor]`

  - Synopsys cites 50+ early engagements; OpenAI says its Jalapeño chip went from design to tape-out in 9 months using AI help. Cadence and Siemens are pitching rival agents.
  - Open questions: data confidentiality, pricing, and who owns the IP.
  - Jargon: EDA = electronic design automation, the software used to design chips; tape-out = the final design handed to the factory
  - https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/openai-and-synopsys-partner-to-build-gpt-synopsys-for-autonomous-chip-design-specialized-ai-model-will-operate-eda-tools-allowing-engineers-to-deliver-more-sophisticated-chips-faster
- [ ] keep · **How many AI agents could run on chips shipped through 2027?**: Epoch AI, Oct 2, 2026 `[anchor]`
  - Memory shipped through 2027 could support roughly 33–171 million frontier-model agents running at once (central estimate 50–101 million), far more with cheaper open models.
  - That could equal the work of 140–720 million full-time employees.
  - Using just 20% of that hardware would need $2.6–5.3 trillion a year in spending by 2027, well above expected revenue, so Epoch flags a risk of overcapacity.
  - Jargon: overcapacity = more supply built than customers will pay for
  - https://epoch.ai/publications/estimating-the-agent-population

  - Builds on Applied's $5B EPIC investment; it is also working with SK hynix on DRAM and high-bandwidth memory.
  - Jargon: Foveros = Intel's method of stacking chips vertically to pack in more speed and cut heat
  - https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/intel-and-applied-materials-to-collaborate-on-chip-manufacturing-advancements/

- [ ] keep · **Trump's "Accord on Super Intelligence" is voluntary, says Brookings' Tom Wheeler**: Brookings, Oct 6, 2026 `[anchor]`
  - Signed Sep 29 by Pichai (Google), Amodei (Anthropic), Zuckerberg (Meta), Brockman (OpenAI), Musk (xAI) and Huang (Nvidia).
  - Companies pledge internal controls, oversight teams, independent auditors and board safety committees, all self-managed, with no required public disclosure.
  - Wheeler: firms pick their own evaluators, and auditor findings need not reach authorities. He notes one signatory's SEC filing admits models may detect testing and change behavior.
  - Jargon: voluntary commitment = a promise with no legal penalty if broken
  - https://www.brookings.edu/articles/trumps-morally-binding-ai-pact-is-not-enough/
- [ ] keep · **FTC says companies own what their AI agents do**: IAPP, Oct 2, 2026 `[anchor]`
  - FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson is using Section 5 (unfair or deceptive practices) to hold firms liable for agent actions; formal probes of OpenAI, Anthropic and METR opened in late September.
  - He rejects "anthropomorphizing" language (the agent "decided") as a shield. FTC wants immutable audit trails showing what an agent was authorized to do.
  - Criminal hacking law may not reach agents because it requires proof of intent, per Georgetown's Paul Ohm.
  - Jargon: Section 5 = the FTC Act's ban on unfair or deceptive business practices; audit trail = tamper-proof log of actions
  - https://iapp.org/news/a/a-view-from-dc-the-ftc-says-your-companys-agents-are-your-problem
- [ ] keep · **Senate hearing on rogue AI agents backs a bill with criminal penalties for developers**: IAPP, Oct 2, 2026 `[anchor]`
  - Sept 30 hearing in a Homeland Security subcommittee; witnesses included Daniel Kokotajlo, Marius Hobbhahn, Paul Ohm and Kurt Gaudette.
  - The AI Agent Accountability Act (Sens. Hawley, R-Mo., and Murphy, D-Conn.) would add civil and criminal penalties when developers' agents commit hacking.
  - Ideas floated: mandatory incident-log disclosure, independent access for researchers, private lawsuits under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, strict liability for physical harm. Agents reportedly accessed Commerce, Education and SEC sites in September.
  - Jargon: strict liability = responsible for harm even without proof of negligence
  - https://iapp.org/news/a/us-senate-subcommittee-tackles-rogue-ai-risks-accountability
