## Source quality and verification
1. Open the actual article, not merely its search-result snippet. Confirm the article date and publication. Check that the link resolves to the story described.
2. Paraphrase only facts supported by that source. Record who says what, including whether figures are forecasts, preliminary estimates or independently reproduced findings.
3. Prefer first-hand research and announcements for launches; label developer-provided benchmarks as the developer's own claims. Use reputable reporting and second sources to evaluate disputes and controversies.
4. For employment and policy, identify the population, region, methodology and whether a proposal is a law, enacted policy or someone's stated position.
5. Do not publish unverified rumors, paywalled headlines without sufficient body evidence, or stale stories without labeling them as catch-up.
6. Skip failed sources and log failures. Never fabricate a claim, URL or source count. Fewer verified stories are better than hitting a quota.

## Never use as stand-alone factual evidence
- Reddit threads, casual social posts, unsourced forum claims
- AI-generated news websites or SEO content farms
- Anonymous rumors, promotional copy presented as independent reporting
- Reposted press releases or articles that conceal the primary source
- Search snippets when the actual article couldn't be read
