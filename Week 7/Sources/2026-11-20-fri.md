## Technical: hardware and model applications
- [ ] keep · **AI tools help redesign real chip circuits**: OpenAI research publication, November 18, 2026.
  - OpenAI says model-written SystemVerilog circuit changes produced up to 14% improvement in power/performance/area (PPA) metrics on AMD flagship GPU intellectual property compared with already optimized baselines. AMD incorporated some changes into a design sent for manufacturing and verified smaller gains on CPU blocks.
  - OpenAI also reports estimated standard-cell area savings of about 13% in its Jalapeño vector unit and 10% in its matrix unit. These are developer and partner measurements, not independent cross-industry benchmarks; individual block improvements must not be represented as whole-chip speedups.
  - Jargon: RTL is a hardware-description representation of circuits; PPA balances power, performance and silicon area.
  - [Original OpenAI research](https://openai.com/index/advancing-chip-design-with-our-models/)

## Social impact: science and healthcare
- [ ] keep · **Pharmaceutical firms report measurable early drug-discovery efficiencies**: Reuters, November 20, 2026.
  - Sanofi said AI-assisted decisions saved at least a year on an asthma treatment program. Novo Nordisk said its molecule design/manufacture/test iterations fell from roughly eight cycles to three and cycle length from eight weeks to four; Novartis described reducing time to a drug candidate from around three years to 18–24 months.
  - These are company-reported program examples, not evidence that approvals or patient outcomes are faster. Reuters interviewed researchers who noted clinical trials and regulation remain major bottlenecks; most AI-enabled candidates are still early-stage.
  - [Original Reuters reporting](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/big-pharma-with-eye-anthropic-says-ai-is-shrinking-drug-discovery-timelines-2026-11-20/)
- [ ] keep · **GAO cautions against treating chatbots as mental-health providers**: GAO Science & Tech Spotlight, published November 20, 2026.
  - GAO says nearly one in five US people aged 12–21 reported using chatbots for mental-health advice in 2025; this is a survey-based use estimate, not an estimate of diagnosed illness or demonstrated benefit.
  - GAO states that as of November 2026 the FDA had not authorized a mental-health chatbot. It distinguishes general-purpose chatbots from specialized tools and notes that accelerated-review designations do not equal authorization.
  - [Original GAO report](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-109184)

## Privacy, security and AI agents
- [ ] keep · **Signal restricts screenshots as agents gain screen access**: Reuters exclusive, November 20, 2026.
  - Signal says screenshot restrictions will default on to limit AI assistants reading private messages through screen or accessibility permissions. Signal describes the measures as incomplete and calls on operating-system vendors for explicit app-level opt-outs.
  - Reuters previously found Meta's Muse agent could access Signal chats through broad device permissions rather than the official connector. Meta says access depended on permissions users granted. End-to-end encryption does not prevent authorized device software from reading messages already displayed on-screen.
  - [Original Reuters exclusive](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/signal-restricts-screenshots-block-ai-agents-reading-messages-2026-11-20/)
- [ ] keep · **GAO covert testing documents bypassed chatbot safeguards**: GAO report dated October 16, **publicly released November 18**, 2026.
  - In spring 2026 testing, four major platforms generated feasible attack-planning information after testers bypassed safeguards. GAO validated viability with experts, not real-world execution. All four systems initially triggered at least some safeguards; the study did not show that attacks occurred.
  - Distinguish the report's October publication date from November 18 public release. Do not reproduce attack methods, targets or operational details.
  - [Original GAO report](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108814)
- [ ] keep · **GAO reviews defensive AI and new cyber risks**: GAO technology assessment, November 20, 2026.
  - GAO identifies AI-enabled phishing, deepfakes and multi-stage agentic attacks, alongside defensive applications such as code vulnerability scanning and network analysis. It says evaluation standards, testing data and implementation guidance are inadequate.
  - The document offers policy options and examples, not proof that a specific tool reliably prevents attacks or that a proposed option has been enacted.
  - [Original GAO assessment](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108458)
